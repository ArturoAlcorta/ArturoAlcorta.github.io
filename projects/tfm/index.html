Exploring Low-Rank Adaptation for Generating Synthetic Autonomous Driving Data

Can we train self-driving perception models without collecting thousands of hours of real-world footage? This thesis investigates whether fine-tuned diffusion models can generate realistic synthetic urban scenes good enough to serve as training data — and how to do it efficiently.

Code not publicly available. This work is part of an ongoing research project. The source code and trained models cannot be shared at this time.

The Problem

Training perception models for autonomous vehicles requires enormous amounts of annotated real-world data — images labeled with every car, pedestrian, and traffic sign in each frame. Collecting and annotating this data is expensive, slow, and doesn't scale. A single annotator can take minutes per image, and edge cases (night rain, unusual intersections) are rare in the wild but critical for safety.

Synthetic data generated by AI models offers a compelling alternative — but only if the generated images are realistic enough, and only if we can produce them efficiently without retraining from scratch every time.

Approach

Step 1 — Finding the Best Image Captions

Diffusion models are guided by text prompts. We first evaluated several image captioning models to determine which one produces descriptions that, when used as prompts, yield the most faithful and realistic urban scene generations.

Step 2 — Fine-tuning Diffusion Models

Using the best captioning strategy, we fine-tuned diffusion models on urban driving datasets under three different strategies: full fine-tuning, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), and AdaLoRA (Adaptive LoRA). Each approach trades off between the number of trainable parameters and the resulting image quality.

Step 3 — Evaluation

Models were assessed using both quantitative metrics — FID, LPIPS, CLIPScore, and CMMD — and qualitative visual inspection. We also studied how hyperparameters like LoRA rank and batch size affect training stability and output quality.

Key Findings

↓

LoRA slashes parameters without sacrificing quality

LoRA significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters while preserving image quality comparable to full fine-tuning — making large-scale synthetic data generation far more accessible.

≈

AdaLoRA doesn't gain much for diffusion models

AdaLoRA, which dynamically allocates parameter budgets across layers, works best for language models. In diffusion models, the attention matrices appear to have similar importance — so AdaLoRA converges to results similar to vanilla LoRA.

↑

Higher LoRA rank improves generation quality

Increasing the LoRA rank — which controls how much capacity the adapter has — tends to improve the visual quality of generated images.

⊞

Larger batches reduce training variability

Larger batch sizes lead to more stable training runs with less variance in final model quality, at the cost of higher memory requirements.

Takeaway

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning — particularly LoRA — is a practical and effective approach for generating realistic synthetic urban scene datasets. These techniques make it feasible to produce large volumes of high-quality training data for autonomous driving perception models, without the cost and logistical burden of real-world data collection.
