Gym Technique Analyzer

Per-rep lifting technique from a single phone video

Upload a set of squats, RDLs or bench press and get every repetition broken down into the numbers that describe it — depth, range of motion, tempo, spinal flexion, knee cave, left/right symmetry — and, optionally, a Dynamic Time Warping comparison of each rep against reference lifters. The analysis engine started life inside trAIner; this is the standalone version of it.

A single frame of the annotated pose.mp4 the analyser writes alongside its numbers — rep 2 of a squat set, caught at the bottom position where the eccentric hands over to the concentric.

All 52 SynthPose keypoints are drawn, including the anatomical spine markers COCO-17 does not have. It is also the protocol framing in practice: shot about 30° off the sagittal plane, which is what keeps both knees in view — and knee cave measurable at all.

Read the comparison mode carefully. The default mode needs no reference lifter at all and stands on its own. The optional DTW mode measures similarity to a specific, small pool of 26 reference repetitions from two lifters — a low score there may be body proportions or tempo rather than a fault.

The Problem

Filming your sets is free and everybody already does it; getting anything out of the footage is the hard part. Watching your own squat back tells you very little beyond "looked fine" — you cannot eyeball how much your spine rounded, whether rep 6 was 200 ms slower out of the hole than rep 1, or whether your left elbow consistently lags your right by 20°.

A pose model can measure all of that frame by frame, so the video you were filming anyway becomes a set of numbers you can track across sessions. Which metrics are worth measuring at all — and which executions are good enough to serve as references — were decided in consultation with a professional strength coach, rather than picked for being easy to compute.

Two Modes, One Score 

score = 0.5 · movement pattern (DTW)  +  0.5 · relational metrics

Technique only (default)

The relational half alone: knee valgus, spine flexion range, hip/knee ROM ratio and hip-knee coordination for the sagittal lifts; left/right symmetry for bench. Two of them are judged against an absolute cutoff needing no reference at all, the rest against the pooled range of the reference reps. No DTW is involved — which is the whole point of the split: an analysis run without the comparison still comes back with a score.

Technique + DTW comparison

Adds the movement-pattern half: each rep is dynamic-time-warped against every reference rep on file, returning the closest match and a per-joint breakdown of where the divergence is. DTW absorbs tempo differences, so a slow rep and a fast rep with the same shape score alike — and matching against individual reps rather than an averaged curve conditions on body type, since the mean of differently-proportioned lifters is a trajectory none of them performs.

The split exists because the halves answer genuinely different questions. "Is my back rounding more than it should?" is a measurement against a range. "Does my hinge look like theirs?" is a shape comparison, and inherits every limitation of the reference pool. When only one half is available the score is that half, rather than a combined number that quietly means something different rep to rep.

The Pipeline

01 Preprocess

Resample to 15 FPS and cap the long side at 1920 px. Consecutive frames of a barbell lift are highly redundant; 15 FPS still resolves a 0.6 s concentric into ~9 samples.

02 Pose

YOLO11m detects people, an IoU tracker keeps the principal one across frames, and SynthPose (ViTPose-Huge, 52 keypoints) estimates the skeleton. SynthPose over stock COCO-17 specifically for the spine: it predicts anatomical markers — C7, T6, T11, L2, ASIS — that COCO simply does not have, and every spine metric below is built on them.

03 Normalize

Per-exercise origin and scale: hip-centred with femur/torso-length scaling for the sagittal lifts, shoulder-centred with shoulder-width scaling for bench. Both use a median over the clip rather than per-frame values, so a single bad frame cannot rescale the skeleton. This is what makes metrics comparable between people of different sizes and between clips shot at different distances.

04 Count reps

A 4-state hysteresis machine per exercise on the primary joint angle, cross-confirmed by a second signal — knee + hip for the squat, hip + torso inclination for the RDL, both elbows for bench. Hysteresis rather than peak-picking, because a lifter pausing mid-rep must not read as two reps. Reps are counted once, and the metrics, the DTW segmentation and the UI numbering all reuse those windows, so rep 3 means the same thing everywhere.

05 Measure

Per-rep metrics from the normalized keypoints. All of them are relations between joints — ratios, ranges, phase — rather than absolute positions, so they survive differences in body proportions and ROM. That is exactly what makes a band pooled across differently-proportioned lifters meaningful at all.

06 Compare (optional)

DTW per joint angle against each reference rep. Distance is the normalized warped distance in degrees; each angle scores 100·(1 − distance/15°), floored at 0 and averaged, and the rep keeps its best match. The 15° denominator is the "totally different movement" anchor. Joint angles rather than raw coordinates, because angles are invariant to mirroring, camera distance and limb length — coordinates would score a tall lifter as "different" for being tall.

07 Score

Each metric becomes a 0–100 sub-score, those average into the relational half, and the DTW best match is the pattern half. Scoring runs when the report is requested rather than at analysis time — so rebuilding a band or changing a weight re-scores every past analysis without touching a video.

What You Get Per Rep

Squat & RDL

  • ◈ Knee cave (verdict)
  • ◈ Spine flexion range
  • ◈ Hip/knee ROM ratio
  • ◈ Hip-knee out of phase %
  • ◈ Hip-knee MARP

Bench press

  • ◈ Elbow L/R asymmetry
  • ◈ Shoulder L/R asymmetry

Plus, on every exercise: the set's headline score and how it was composed, per-rep tempo chips (total / eccentric / concentric), any cues raised, and — in comparison mode — the closest reference with its per-joint bars.

Spine flexion range — the mid-hip → L2 → C7 angle is ~180° with a neutral spine and closes as the back rounds. Reported as the range within the rep, so it measures how much you rounded rather than each person's individual neutral posture.

Hip/knee ROM ratio — hip range divided by knee range. A high ratio is a hip-dominant hinge; a low one on an RDL means you are squatting it.

Hip-knee MARP — mean absolute relative phase via a Hilbert transform, plus out-of-phase %: whether hip and knee move together or one leads. 0° is perfect synchrony.

Knee cave — ankle width over knee width across the deepest quarter of the rep. Differential, because that cancels the common-mode perspective offset of an angled camera; at the bottom, because valgus appears under load at depth.

Design Decisions

⌖

3D lifting was tried and abandoned

The complete answer is to lift the 2D keypoints into a 3D skeleton. The lift models did not preserve limb length through the movement — the quadriceps stretched and shrank by as much as 70% between phases of the same rep. Metrics built on segment lengths and joint angles cannot survive that.

∠

One camera at a deliberately intermediate angle

Several cameras, one per plane, is a real imposition on someone filming themselves between sets. So: a single camera at 30° off sagittal for squat and RDL, 45° between the frontal and transverse planes for bench. The angle is what makes both knees visible — and therefore knee cave measurable at all.

!

The metric checks its own geometry

Footage shot to protocol measures ~0.58 on the ankle-separation test; dead side-on sits at 0.005–0.07. Before a 0.15 cutoff existed, one such clip reported a capped, severe knee cave built entirely out of about a pixel of jitter — dividing two near-zero widths amplifies noise without bound. Knee cave now returns n/a rather than a number it cannot justify.

⇄

The visible leg is picked by shank length, not confidence

Keypoint confidence is not a proxy for visibility: on one squat the model scored the occluded leg 10.6% higher. Median projected shank length separates near from far leg by 12–20%, and holds its orientation through all three lifts. Cross-matched sides, a rep scored 68 against itself; on the same leg, 100.

⛓

Landmark choice is a fallback chain, not a contest

L_Hip (COCO) is the hip joint centre; l_ASIS is the iliac spine, centimetres forward and above it — different points, not competing estimates of one. Preferring whichever scored higher per frame put 47° steps into the hip-angle series. COCO first, marker only when occluded.

≠

Knee cave is judged per exercise

A squat drives the knees out, so parity with the feet is already a fault (flagged above 0.90); an RDL is a narrow stance with soft knees over the feet, landing at 1.0–1.15 as plain geometry (flagged above 1.20). One shared cutoff told all five reference RDL reps their knees were caving in.

Architecture

FastAPI + HTMX frontend, a Celery worker for the CV pipeline, Postgres for the analysis index and per-analysis artifacts on disk. Pipeline stages are pushed to the browser over Server-Sent Events from a Redis pub/sub channel per analysis. The engine itself has no FastAPI, SQLAlchemy or Celery imports anywhere in it — every service takes data or paths in and hands data or paths back, which is what makes it runnable from a notebook as easily as from the worker. 

video upload → FastAPI (POST /analyses) → Celery task
                                    │
    preprocess → pose → normalize → count reps → measure → [compare]
                                    │
                                    ├──→ Redis pub/sub (stage) → SSE → browser
                                    │
                                    └──→ Postgres (status) + JSON artifacts / pose.mp4 on disk

The Reference Set, Honestly

The comparison mode is only as good as what it compares against, and right now that is 26 reps from two lifters — with RDL resting on a single athlete's five reps. Since best-of-references matching works by finding the reference rep most like you, a pool that small mostly cannot contain anyone built like you. More lifters beats more reps per lifter, and a script is included to turn any video of a good set into new reference files through the exact same pipeline a submission goes through.

The reference band — the pooled acceptable range of each relational metric — is generated, never authored. It means nothing apart from the particular implementation of the metrics that produced it, so changing how a metric is computed requires regenerating it, or every rep gets scored against a range that no longer describes the same quantity.
